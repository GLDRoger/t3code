# Kin

Kin is where your company works together: the owner, co-owners, teammates and AI agents, in one
chat list. Agents are contacts like anyone else. You ask them for things in plain words, and they
keep Tasks, Money, Clients and Docs current for you.

Kin has five places: **Chats**, **Tasks**, **Money**, **Clients** and **Docs**. On the phone they
are the dock; on the web and the desktop app they sit at the top of the sidebar, which in a narrow
window slides in from the menu button at the top left. `⌘1` to `⌘5` (`Ctrl` on Windows and Linux)
open them.
**Settings** is the gear at the bottom left on the web and the desktop app, and the gear on Chats on
the phone and in a narrow window. It opens over whatever you're looking at; close it to carry on
where you were. What you see in each depends on your place in the team; see
[Who sees and does what](#who-sees-and-does-what).

Settings has **You** (your profile, notifications and appearance) for everyone, and for the owner and co-owners
**Company** (Team, Agents, Check-ins, Connections, Deals, Memory, Company details, Approvals, and
Recommendations for the owner himself). A member with **Can see Clients** has a Company group with
Deals only, to read the company's deal stages. On the
computer Kin runs on, **This computer** holds the app's own settings, such as providers and models,
and how much of each provider's usage your agents have spent.

## Getting started

### Joining a team

Someone who runs the company sends you a join link. Open it in a browser, or in the Kin phone app:
on its first screen, tap **I have a join link**. You're in straight away, with your chats and the
places the owner turned on for you.

A join link works once and lasts a week. If yours has expired or was used, ask for a new one.

### Your devices

Once you're in, you add your other devices yourself. Open **Your devices** (under your name on the
web, in **Settings** on the phone) and choose **Link a device**. Kin shows a link and a QR code that
work once, for 10 minutes. Scan the QR code with the new phone, or open the link in the new
browser. Each time a device is linked, Kin tells you in your chat with the Team agent; without a
Team agent, in your direct chats. Your phones with notifications on also get an alert, when phone
alerts are set up for your company's Kin. If no notice reaches you, check **Your devices** for any
you didn't add.

A linked device stays signed in for 30 days, then you link it again the same way. You can have 10
devices at a time. Remove a device from the same list to sign it out at once, along with every
device linked from it and the coding agent keys made on them. Signing a device out from **Sharing &
access** does the same. On a phone, **Sign out of** your team under **Settings → This phone** forgets the team on that
phone, on every computer the phone holds for it, and asks your Kin to stop that team's
notifications there. If a computer doesn't confirm, because the phone is offline or Kin doesn't
answer, the phone says so once, and its notifications may keep coming until the phone is removed
from your devices. The phone stays in your list until it is removed. A device you linked can
remove itself and the devices linked from it, but not the device you joined with: for that one, ask
whoever runs your Kin. The owner can see and remove a teammate's devices on the **Team** page.

### Your profile

Open **Settings → Profile** to set your
name, skills, time zone and working hours, and in **How to talk to me** how you like agents to
write to you ("short, numbers first, nothing after 8 pm"). Only you can set that line. Agents read
your profile when they decide who should do something, when to message you and how. An agent nudges you about the same thing at most once a day,
inside your hours.

Your **Quiet hours** (22:00 to 07:00 unless you change them) run on your own clock. Nothing an
agent sends you unprompted arrives in them: a message passed on from a teammate, a nudge, a note
about a task. With **Hold non-urgent messages outside my working hours** on, which it is for
everyone but the owner, those also wait outside your working hours. They arrive in order when your
day starts, and whoever sent one is told when you'll get it. A reply to something you wrote in the
last ten minutes, a teammate writing to you directly, and anything urgent arrive at once. Your
phone rings by the same rule, except that a teammate's direct message in your quiet hours rings
only when they end, once per teammate. Only you can change these two.

**Settings → Notifications** sets how this device tells you about new messages. On the web and the
desktop app it alerts you while Kin is open in a window you aren't looking at; on the phone it opens
the phone's own notification settings. On Android those list Kin's kinds apart (Messages, Questions
and approvals, Briefs, Devices and security), so you can silence one and keep the rest.

### Adding a teammate (owner and co-owners)

Choose **New**, then **Add teammate**, and give their name. Kin makes a link for you to send; it
doesn't send it for you and won't show it again. Make the link from the address your team uses to
reach Kin, not from `localhost`, or it won't open on anyone else's device.

Each teammate starts as a **Member**. Open them under **Settings → Team** to make them a **Co-owner**, to
turn on what they can see (Money, Clients, Docs), to restrict who they can message, or to
**Remove access**.

Removing access signs them out everywhere and changes nothing else. Their open tasks stay in their
name and show under **Needs you** for you to give to someone else; no agent picks them up. To bring
them back, choose **Make a new link** on their row: they return as the same person, with their
role, places, chats and tasks. Only the owner's link brings back a co-owner as a co-owner.

## Talking to an agent

Open an agent in Chats and write to it as you would to a colleague: "what's late this week?",
"remind me to call Orbit at 4", "draft the November invoice for Acme". It answers in the same chat.

- **Each agent has a character.** Finance is exact and wary of promises, Growth sees upside
  everywhere, Social lives online. An agent has views, says when it disagrees with you, and then
  does what you decide. To change how one sounds, tell it in its chat ("be warmer with me"); it
  rewrites its character and says so in a line. The character is a private doc in **Docs**, and its
  history brings an older one back. A character never changes what an agent may do.
- **It does, then tells.** Inside the company an agent acts and tells you in a line: it moves a
  task, drafts a doc, fixes a record. It asks only what only you know, once, with the default it
  will take. Say "undo it" and it puts back what it just changed. To let it do something without
  asking every time, say so ("Finance may always draft payment reminders"); it remembers.
- **One reply per message.** Each message gets one answer. If the agent starts something longer,
  such as a coding job, it says so and comes back in the chat when there's news.
- **It works with what you can see.** An agent answering you sees only what you may see.
  When a member asks about money, it won't share figures they can't see. A message you ask it to
  pass on with a sum the teammate can't see comes back to you with that said: send it without the
  figure, or share it with them first.
- **Long answers read like a page.** A short reply is a bubble. One with headings, a table or a
  list of several things fills the chat's width, in sections you can fold; on the phone a long one
  opens with **Read more**. A check-in arrives as a brief: a greeting, the date, then only the
  sections that have something in them. Charts and figures appear right where they belong in the
  answer. A question or **Confirm** card sits at the foot of the answer; after you tap, the
  result shows on that same card.
- **Names open what they name.** An agent, a teammate, a client, a project, a task or a document
  number such as INV-1 in a message is a link to it, when you may open it. Touch and hold one on
  the phone, or hover on the web, to see its state first.
- **Attach files.** Anyone in the company can attach pictures, PDFs and documents in a chat they
  can write in: their chat with an agent, a chat with a teammate, or a group they are in. Only
  people in that chat can open them, and an agent reads them with the access of whoever sent them.
- **It asks with cards.** A question with choices, a chart, or something to confirm comes as a card
  in the chat. Tapping an answer answers the agent; it is never an approval. An answer with a line
  under it, such as Mark W-4 “Set up the API” done, does exactly that when you tap it, with no
  wait for the agent. If what it names changed since, the card says nothing was done. A reaction
  never does it, and Kin never takes such an answer as a default: it waits for your tap.
  On a phone, the notice of an agent's question with two answers lists them as 1 and 2; press
  and hold it (on Android, expand it) and tap **Answer 1** or **Answer 2** to answer without
  finding the chat. The phone
  unlocks, opens the card and answers it once it reaches your Kin. Something to confirm, and a
  question with more answers, opens the card instead.
- **It asks once, and says what it will do.** An agent's question comes with the answer it will go
  with and the day, by default the end of your next working day (until 19:30). If you don't answer
  by then, it goes with that answer and tells you in one line; say "undo it" to reverse. Something
  that would send, post or move money has no such default: it is raised once more the next working
  morning and then waits for you. Another agent won't ask you the same thing while it is open.
  To change the answer before the day, tap another one. You can also answer in words in the chat
  it asked in: only that agent takes it from what you wrote, the card says so, and Undo beside it
  opens the question again. It then waits for your answer with no default, and anything already
  done from the first answer stands.
- **It remembers.** Ask it to remember something and it keeps it. "Keep this between us" keeps it
  for the owner alone.
- **It comes back later.** "Remind me Friday at 10" or "check on this in two hours" sets a reminder.
  Anyone can ask for one in their own chat with an agent, and it arrives there, at that time, for
  them alone. "Every weekday at 9, tell me what's late" sets a repeating one, shown as a card you can **Run
  now**, **Pause**, **Edit** or **Delete**. Only the owner and co-owners can set repeating ones.
- **It passes things on.** Ask one agent about another's area and it hands it over and tells you
  so. The request shows in the other agent's chat as **Sent by** the first, and the answer comes back
  to the agent you asked, in your chat with it. Ask it to tell a teammate something and it arrives
  in their chat as **Sent by** you, via the agent, and their answer comes back to you the same way.
- **It does engineering work.** An agent that looks after engineering reads your GitHub repos, pull
  requests and checks, reviews pull requests, and starts coding work in T3 Code. It doesn't merge:
  when a pull request is approved and green, it tells you it's ready and you merge it. See
  [Who agents work as](#who-agents-work-as) for what holds it to that.

**Waiting on you** in Chats collects what needs your answer. An agent's open question shows there with
its default and day, and you can answer it right there.

### An agent's page

Tap an agent's name at the top of its chat, or in any message, to open its page. It's also under
**Settings → Agents** and in the command palette. The owner and co-owners can change
everything there; anyone else sees a short card with what you wrote under **What teammates see**
and a way to message it.

- **Model.** Each agent runs on one model, with one effort, for everything it does. A change
  applies from its next reply. Medium or High effort is recommended: Low is too shallow, and
  anything above High tends to over-think. You can also say it in a chat: "run Finance on Sonnet". New agents start on the company default, set under
  **Settings → Agents**. If a model goes away from your server, the agent runs on the
  default and its page says so. The page also shows what the agent has spent this month.
- **Character.** Read it, **Edit** it, open its **History** to bring an older one back, or **Ask it
  to rewrite itself**, which starts a message in its chat for you to finish.
- **Pause** stops an agent taking turns until you **Resume** it; messages and check-ins wait.
  **Retire agent** removes it from Chats, and **Restore** brings it back with its history.
- The page also shows its check-ins and quiet hours, who may message it, what it can reach, its
  playbooks in **Docs**, and the tasks it has open.

When you create an agent, choose its model and write its character there, or tick **Write one for
me** and the Chief of staff drafts one with you.

### What an agent never does without you

An agent prepares; a person decides. These wait for the owner's or a co-owner's **Confirm** on a
card, and typing "yes" in the chat doesn't count:

- sending or replying to an email, or saving a calendar invite;
- posting on any social account, the company's own included, or commenting publicly on someone
  else's repository;
- recording what a bank statement shows (see [Bank and card statements](./kin-statements.md));
- recording money received for a receipt that has no payment yet;
- changing the company details.

Changing what an agent may do needs more: an approval with Face ID on a
phone set up under **Settings → Approvals** in the Kin app. Until one is set up, nothing that needs
approval can happen.

## Messaging a teammate or an agent

Anyone can message anyone else on the team: **New → Message someone**, or **Message** on their
profile. It is a chat for the two of you, with no agent. Only you and they can read it, not the owner
or a co-owner, and the chat says so at its top. Starting it again opens the same chat.

**Message someone** also lists every agent you may message, with what teammates see about it. Your
chat with an agent is your own; whoever runs the workspace can see it.

Everyone can message every agent and everyone else, unless the owner or a co-owner restricts a pair.
Restrict a person and an agent from either one's page (**Who can message** on the agent, or the
agent's row on the person), and two people from the person's page with **Restrict messaging
with…**. A restriction holds both ways; their chat stays, to read only, until you **Lift** it.
**Settings → Team → Who can message** lists every restriction. Owners and co-owners can't be
restricted.

## Group chats and the company chat

A group is one chat with any mix of agents and people (owner and co-owners: **New → New group**).
The first agent you pick answers when a message names nobody; write `@` and a name to ask another.
Agents keep to one short message in a group, and don't talk to each other unless you name one.
People in a group can ask its agents to talk things through; only the owner and co-owners can have an agent
start work there. Anyone can give an agent a task in Tasks, and it then works with that person's
access, never more.

In a group, an agent won't show something to people who can't see it. Ask about an invoice there
and it answers you privately instead.

The **company chat** is one group with the whole team and your agents. Turn it on under **Settings →
Team** and pick which agent answers when nobody is named.

The **team agent**, set under **Settings → Team**, is the one every teammate can chat with. It sees only
what each person may see.

### The Team room

Your agents settle things between them in the **Team room**: say "sort it out with Finance", or an
agent opens a discussion itself when something crosses into another agent's area. Only you and
co-owners can read it; teammates never see it, so exact figures can be said there. It is muted and
never counts as unread; a small dot means something new.

- Whose call it is decides. Agents with a stake may speak once uninvited, a decision can be objected
  to once, and every discussion ends: decided, parked, or brought to you.
- You hear the outcome once, as one line in the chat you asked in, with **See how they got there**.
  When an agent started it, the line comes with your next check-in, or, with no check-in set, once
  your quiet hours are over.
- A real disagreement comes as one card with both sides, the Chief of staff's lean and two choices.
  One from a discussion an agent started waits for the same moment.
- Agents act inside the company, then tell you what they changed. Say "undo it" and they reverse
  those changes. Anything that leaves the company, such as an email, still waits for your Confirm.
- Agents start at most 6 discussions a day and have 2 open at once; a discussion you ask for always
  starts.

## Tasks

Tasks holds everything someone owes someone. Everyone's list reads the same way, so you can tell at
a glance what waits on whom: **On me** is what you have to do; **On others** is what waits on a
teammate, under each person; **On agents** is what an agent has; then everything else, by project.
Each task says who has it first, agents by name, then when it's due and its project.

**On me** starts with what can't wait, each marked **Late**, **Due today** or **Stuck**. For
teammates, the Tasks badge counts your late and due-today tasks. When one of your tasks is late or
due today, Kin tells you in your own chat, counting a private task without naming it; teammates
and co-owners get this plain notice from Kin, while the Chief of staff tells its own owner itself.

The owner and co-owners also see **Needs attention** at the top, for what wants attention across
the company: other people's late or stuck work, quiet clients, projects with nothing moving. Each
task shows in one place only, so your own stay under On me. Choose **I've got it** when you're
handling one. When nothing needs attention, the section isn't there.

- **New task** gives a task a title, a due day and someone to do it. Ask an agent instead and it
  writes the task for you.
- **Give to someone else** moves a task to a teammate or an agent. They get it in their Tasks and a
  message in their chat.
- **Hand back** returns a task you were given to the person who gave it, with a reason if you like.
  When someone asked an agent to give it to you, that person gave it ("Zoya gave this to you, via
  Chief of staff"), and it goes back to them.
- **Who can see this** says who can see a task. **People on it and the project** is the usual.
  **Only me** makes it private: it hides the task from other teammates, not from the owner and
  co-owners. Only they and whoever has it see it, and it stays out of every list, count and search
  for anyone else. A project lead who marks one can undo it; others see whose it is, as
  **Only Dev Malhotra**. Members can't change it.
- **Add a link** under **Linked** keeps a web page with a task, such as its design file, with a
  name if you like. Whoever can change the task adds or removes one; everyone who sees it opens it.

Keep amounts, rates and client terms out of task titles and notes. Project people read those; put
the figures in the quote or the invoice.

### Your tasks in your own coding agent

A coding agent key lets your own coding agent, such as Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, read the tasks
you hold, add notes to them, and set them to to-do, in progress or done. It can't reach anyone
else's tasks, anything labelled sensitive or under a client or project labelled sensitive, or any
chats, money or files.

To make one, open **Settings → Profile** and choose **Make a
key** under **Coding agent keys**. Name it for the computer or tool it's for. Kin shows the key, its
address and ready-to-paste setup for common coding agents once; copy them before you close it. You
can hold three keys at a time, and each works for 30 days. If your coding agent's computer reaches
Kin at a different address than the app you made the key in, use that address with the same ending.

Each setup keeps the key in the coding agent's own settings, so it works in every session:

- **Claude Code:** run the one line Kin shows in a terminal.
- **Codex:** add the three lines Kin shows to the end of `~/.codex/config.toml`, then start a new
  Codex session.
- **Cursor:** add the block Kin shows to `~/.cursor/mcp.json`, beside any servers already there.

To check it worked, ask your coding agent to list your Kin tasks; the key's row then says when it
was last used. If you set Codex up before with a `bearer_token_env_var` line, remove that line from
the `[mcp_servers.kin]` section and add the `http_headers` line Kin shows instead.

Choose **Remove** beside a key to stop it; the coding agent loses access on its next call. The owner
and co-owners can see and remove a teammate's keys on their profile, and removing someone's access
ends all of their keys. Removing the device a key was made on ends that key too. A key made before
keys were tied to a device has ended; make a new one.

## Clients and projects

Clients has three lists: Clients, Deals and Projects. Clients lists each client and prospect, with
when you last spoke to them and what's next.
On a client, add contacts and **Log contact** after a call or meeting. If nothing is logged for a
while, the client shows up under Needs attention.

A project has a lead, a target day, milestones and its tasks. Its page says whether it's on track,
what's late or stuck, and who's on it. An overdue invoice for it, or no contact with its client
for longer than their check-in days (`client_checkin_days`, 14 by default), puts it at risk. Only
invoices linked to the project count; Kin never guesses which project an invoice is for. The
owner, co-owners and the project's lead add milestones;
everyone on the project sees them. The owner or a co-owner sets the lead under **Project lead**.

**People** on a project's page lists who's on it. The owner, co-owners and the project's lead choose
**Add people** to put teammates and agents on it, and take someone off from the same list. The owner
can also do this from a teammate's page under **Team**, and choose people when making a project.
Whoever is added or made lead gets a message in their chat with a link to the project. Taken off,
its tasks leave their list straight away. A member can't add themselves.

**Note for every task** on a project's page is one note its tasks show at the top of their
Activity, as **From the project**: the brand colours, the client's working hours. The owner and
co-owners write, change or clear it there; clearing it takes it off every task at once. Only people
who can see the project read it on a task.

A member sees client names and contacts only when the owner turns on **Can see Clients**, and never
what a client is charged or owes. With it on, they also find the projects they're on, under their
clients.

### Deals

A deal is a sale you're working on with a client or prospect: what it's worth, when you expect it
to close, and who looks after it. It moves through your company's stages (New, In talks, Proposal
and Deciding until you change them), then is won or lost. Winning a deal with a prospect makes the
prospect a client. Losing one keeps a one-line reason. A won or lost deal can be reopened; it goes
back to its last stage, and a client stays a client.

A new deal names its first next step, a follow-up for whoever looks after it, or says there is none
yet. Kin tells whoever looks after a deal when it has no next step, when it has gone quiet (14 days
with nothing done on it, unless you change that) and when it's past its expected day. Totals are
kept per currency and never converted.

The owner and co-owners set the stages and the quiet days in **Settings → Deals**; removing a stage
moves its open deals to the stage you pick. A member reaches Deals only with **Can see Clients**
on. They see every deal except private ones and those under a private client, add deals on the
clients they see, and change the deals they look after. Only the owner and co-owners mark a deal
private. A deal is handed on only to someone who can see it, and the open next steps its keeper
held go with it.

To bring deals in from a spreadsheet or another CRM, the owner or a co-owner uses **Import a file**
in Clients.

### What we hear

When a prospect raises a worry, such as price or timing, an agent notes it on the deal as a short
label with the words it heard, and the deal lists it under **Concerns raised**. **What we hear**, in
Deals, counts how many deals raised each concern and how many of those are still open, won or lost,
so you can see which worries cost you sales. Open a concern to see the deals that raised it. Counts
cover only the deals you can see.

### Campaigns and marketing lists

A campaign is a project marked **Campaign**. Choose it in **Came from** on a prospect or client,
and the campaign's page counts the prospects and clients that came from it, their deals and how
many were won. Give a campaign an **Audience** tag and its list is every contact carrying that tag.

Tags are your company's own words, added to contacts on their client's page; a contact takes up to 20. A tag's list is every contact carrying it, and the Clients list also gives the contacts at the
clients it shows. A list is worked out again each time you open it, so it's never out of date.
**Export list** saves it as a CSV file for your mail tool. Lists and results hold only what you can
see in Clients. Tag lists and campaign results need **Can see Clients**; a project's lead without it
lists and exports their clients' contacts by name only, with no emails, phone numbers or tags.

## Money

Money shows what you're owed, what's overdue, who owes you, the next 30 days, cash in the bank and
each month. **Needs attention** at the top lists overdue invoices and renewals coming up.

A new company chooses its country first: the owner or a co-owner sets it in **Settings → Company
details**, and its currency follows, with its financial year and tax name where Kin knows them.
Until then Kin records no money, and agents say so.

Most of the bookkeeping happens by telling an agent: "Acme paid ₹48,250 today", "we spent $120 on
hosting", "the GST filing is due on the 20th". It records the entry and answers in a line.

- **Quotes.** Ask for a quote and an agent makes it, numbered, from the company's template. It stays
  a draft until it's sent. When the client says yes, choose **Make invoice** on the quote, or ask for
  the invoice; it carries the quote's lines over, and the quote then shows which invoice it became.
- **Invoices.** Open an invoice and choose **Make its PDF**; the PDF is filed in Docs and shows on the
  invoice. **Record payment** marks money received; an invoice paid in full becomes Paid. A draft
  isn't owed yet, so **Issue** it before recording a payment. When an invoice goes overdue, the agent drafts a reminder in
  the same email thread and waits for your Confirm.
- **Receipts.** A receipt is made for each payment. For money received before any invoice, use
  **Add → New receipt**, then **Set against** an invoice of the same client when it's issued.
- **Statements.** Send your bank or card statement in a chat to match its rows to invoices and
  expenses. See [Bank and card statements](./kin-statements.md).
- **Paid by a partner.** When a partner pays a company expense from their own money, set **Funded
  by** on the expense to that partner. Leave it empty when the company paid. Its **Reimbursement**
  reads **owed** until the company pays the partner back. Then set it to **repaid**, with the day
  in **Repaid on**. Choose **not reimbursable** when the partner won't be paid back. Expenses imported
  with a partner's email address but no Kin person to match show as owed to that address.
- **Subscriptions.** Tell an agent what you pay for each month or year, with a screenshot or the
  bill, and it keeps them under Subscriptions with what renews next.
- **Budgets and goals.** **Add → Set a budget** or **Add goal**. Goals show to the whole team.
- **Coming-up dates** lists renewals, dues and milestones in one calendar.
- **Currencies.** Every amount stays in its own currency, and totals are kept per currency. Set
  **Exchange rates** to see an "about" figure beside foreign amounts; invoices, receipts and exports
  always keep their own currency.

Money is for the owner and co-owners. A project's lead sees the money filed under the projects they
lead. A member sees no amounts unless the owner turns on **Can see Money**, and then only read-only
figures the owner shares with the team.

Moving from another system? See [Moving your records into Kin](./kin-import.md).

## Docs and how we work

Docs holds what's written for someone to read: briefs, specs, plans, notes, and the PDFs Kin makes.
Each doc is filed under a client, a project or the company; **Unfiled** shows what still needs a
home; a doc an agent writes for a project is filed under it. Ask an agent to write or change a doc
and it edits only the sections that change. Every version is kept: open **History** to see what
changed and, as owner or co-owner, restore an older one.

A doc is private when it's marked private or it's a quote. Otherwise a project's people can read the
project's docs.

**Playbooks** are how your company works: taking on a client, running a project, chasing money,
reviewing code, and so on. Agents read the right playbook before they act. Change one by saying so
in a chat: "chase invoices after 7 days" and the agent edits the playbook and tells you what
changed. A teammate taps **Suggest a change** on the playbook: it opens their chat with the team agent
with the playbook attached, and the suggestion goes to the owner as a question.

Playbooks, like every company doc, are for the whole team to read: everyone with **Can see Docs**,
and the agents working for them. Mark one private to keep it to the owner and co-owners; it then
leaves teammates' lists and search, and agents working for them can't open it either.

## Email and connections

Owners and co-owners connect services under **Settings → Connections**: Google or Microsoft mail,
GitHub, and others. Each mail account reads, and sends only on your Confirm. Tell an agent what an
account is for ("accounts@ is for invoices") and it sends from the right one. A reply always goes
from the account and thread the email is in.

Google and Microsoft each need a sign-in client set up once; Settings shows the steps. If your
company already has one for a mail app (a Google client, or a Microsoft app with a Web redirect and
a client secret), use it instead: add the return address Settings shows and paste its ID and secret.

The card shows exactly what will be sent, from which account, to whom. One **Confirm** sends it
once. Only the owner or a co-owner can confirm. To fix a mail's words yourself, tap **Edit**:
change To, Cc, Subject or the message, then **Send edited mail** sends it as you left it, with
no new draft from the agent. **Save** keeps your words on the card without sending, marked as
edited by you, and its **Confirm** then sends them; leaving the chat or the app with changes saves
them too. If the card changed meanwhile, your words stay on that device until the page is reloaded
or the app restarts, and show again on the card for you to check and save. **Discard edits** puts
the agent's draft back. Attached files go as they were.

If an earlier try at a mail may already have gone (the account gave no clear answer, or Kin
restarted while sending), every card for that mail says so, in any chat. Check the account's sent
mail, then tick **I've checked the sent mail** to Confirm. From Tasks, open the card with
**Review**.

### GitHub

GitHub connects as your own login on your Kin's computer, from the phone or the web: **Settings →
Connections → GitHub → Connect**, then **Get a code**. Tap **Open GitHub**, enter the code, and tap
**Authorize** (grant your organizations if GitHub asks). Kin then shows who it is connected as and
which organizations it reaches. Git and T3 Code on that computer act as you from then on. If you'd
rather not use a code, paste a classic token with `repo`, `read:org` and `workflow` instead.

One account is connected at a time; to switch, **Disconnect** and connect again. Disconnect signs
Kin's computer out; to end it on GitHub too, revoke GitHub CLI under GitHub's Settings →
Applications. If GitHub stops accepting the login, the row says so and offers **Reconnect**.

Once GitHub is connected, an agent works on GitHub in chats only you and co-owners can read. It clones, branches, commits, pushes, opens pull requests and issues, reviews and comments. On your
own and your organizations' repos it just does it, and it asks first before acting in someone
else's repo. Its `gh` and `git` refuse merging, approving, dismissing reviews, pushing to a repo's
default branch or pushing tags, changing a repo's settings, secrets or access, and deleting repos or
releases; it asks you to do those. Longer work runs as a task in its own checkout, and when it finishes the agent replies
once with what changed and the pull request. Teammates' chats and shared rooms still get no shell: what they ask for comes to you.

#### Who agents work as

If your Kin has its own GitHub App, agents' commits, pushes, pull requests, comments and reviews go
out as that App, so blame and history show what an agent did; each commit carries the agent's name
(`Kin-Agent: Tech lead`). On repos the App isn't installed on, and without an App, they go out as
you. When GitHub refuses the App, or you say "do it as me", the agent runs that one command as you
and says so. Memory commits are by the App too. The App's tokens can't change a repo's settings,
but GitHub would let them merge or approve: what stops an agent is its own `gh` and `git`, which run
in a shell that can also read those tokens. To be sure the App changes no default branch, protect
the branches from Kin, as below.

**Settings → Connections → GitHub** says how many watched repos have a default branch Kin's App
isn't kept out of, and each repo in the watched list says whether its branch is unprotected (and
whether the App is on that repo to change it), needs a review first, protected by Kin, protected by
your own rules, not checked yet, or why Kin can't tell. A required review alone doesn't count as
protection: once someone approves, the App can merge, so those repos still offer **Protect**. Your
own rules count only when they restrict updates and their bypass list can't include the App (Kin
can't tell whether a write or maintain role, or a team, covers an App, so those don't count).
GitHub offers no protection on a private repo on its free plan, and only a repo's admins can see or
set it. Protecting a repo the App isn't on yet covers the day you add it. **Protect** on a repo,
or **Protect all**, adds one ruleset on GitHub with your login: only the repo's admins (and an
organization's owners) may update, delete or force-push the default branch. You keep merging your
own pull requests and pushing as before; the App can't change the branch by any route, and no review
is required, so you can't lock yourself out. Kin checks again with each repo refresh, every few
hours. **Remove Kin's protection** deletes only the ruleset Kin made; protection you set up yourself
is never Kin's to change. It guards against the App only: when an agent works as you, Kin's guard on
its `gh` and `git` is what stands.

To give your Kin an App, open **Settings → Connections → GitHub → Create Kin's GitHub App** (owner
and co-owners). Kin suggests a name, GitHub opens with everything filled in, and after you choose
**Create GitHub App** you land back in Kin with the App working; then install it from the account
rows below. The section says which App agents use and who set it up when. **Disconnect the App**
makes Kin forget it and its key, and links to the App on GitHub if you want to delete it there. An
App set up on Kin's computer instead is changed there; your host guide covers that.

**Settings → Connections → GitHub** has a row for each of your accounts and organizations with
watched repos, saying whether the App is on all its repositories, only some, or none. To cover
everything, tap the row's button: it opens the exact GitHub page, where you choose **All
repositories** and save; repos you make later are covered too, and the row updates within a minute.
To keep the App to some repos instead, choose **Only select repositories** there; then **Choose
repos** on the row lets you switch it on or off per repo, or add or remove all watched repos at
once, from Kin, with your GitHub login (GitHub asks for admin on each repo; if it refuses, Kin says
why and links to the page). Each repo in the watched list says whether agents work there as the App
or as you. Only an organization's owners can put the App on it; if you aren't one, GitHub sends
them your request. An App that is private to one account can't go on another until you make it public in its
settings on GitHub; then choose **Check again** on its row. The section also lists the last few things done as you.

#### When things happen on GitHub

Kin hears at once about repos its GitHub App is on, and about your other watched repos within a
minute, from its own checks with your login. When your Kin has a public address, Kin sets up the
webhook of an App it made; for an App it didn't make, choose **Let Kin set it up** under **How fast
Kin hears from GitHub**, on the web or desktop. It leaves a webhook that points somewhere else alone.
If **Settings → Connections → GitHub** lists events to tick, tick them once on GitHub; it links to
the page. On your repos and your organizations' repos, these start an agent on their own:

- **A teammate opens a pull request**, or marks a draft ready: it is reviewed and the review posted.
  On your own pull requests the review is a comment, since GitHub doesn't let you approve your own.
- **New commits on a pull request Kin reviewed**: one more review of what changed, five minutes
  after the last push.
- **A call in a comment, an issue or a line comment**: a line that starts with `/kin`, or with an
  agent's name and a comma ("Tech lead, rename this"), which goes to that agent. On an issue the
  agent says what it will do, works on a branch and opens a pull request that closes the issue. On a
  pull request it pushes a commit to that pull request's branch and replies in the thread, or says it
  can't push there and offers a pull request instead.
- **The `kin` label** on an issue, which works the issue the same way.
- **A review requested from you**, and **failing checks on an agent's own pull request**, which it
  fixes or explains.

Only people GitHub lets write to the repo can call an agent or start one with the `kin` label.
Anyone else's `/kin` starts nothing, and their pull request gets one comment review from its diff
and nothing else: their code never runs, and that review is written knowing nothing private. An agent never reacts
to its own posts, and a mention of you doesn't call it unless you tell it in chat to let mentions
of you call it in a repo. Everything an agent posts on GitHub ends with its name ("_Tech lead, via
Kin_"). It tells you once, in one line with the link; quiet hours hold that line, not the work. A
thread that keeps calling an agent gets five turns an hour, then one comment saying it has stopped
until the hour is up.

Kin watches every repo yours or your organizations' unless you say "leave acme/site alone" in chat.
**Settings → Connections → GitHub** shows how many repos it watches, when it last checked and how
much it handled today; **Show repos** lists them with a switch each, and **Watch every repo** undoes
the switches. Forks of other people's repos start off. If a check fails, the row says why and how to
fix it.

To try pull requests in a browser on Ubuntu 23.10 or later, run `t3 browser setup` once as root.

### Social media

An agent whose domain is social media (Social) runs your company's accounts and each client's
through one posting service, [Zernio](https://zernio.com). Each group (your own accounts, or one
client's) is one Zernio profile with its own key, so one client's posts can never reach another's
accounts.

To connect a group: in Zernio, make a profile for it and connect its accounts (or connect them later
with **Send a connect link**), then make an API key limited to that profile. In Kin, open **Settings →
Connections → Social accounts → Connect accounts**, pick **Our own accounts** or the client, paste the
key, tap **Check the key**, look over the accounts it lists, and tap **Connect these accounts**. A key
that reaches more than one profile is refused. If an account needs reconnecting or the key stops
working, the row says so; **New key** and **Remove** are beside it. Only you and co-owners see this.

Social reads last week's numbers and drafts the week, checking each caption and picture against the
platform's limits. It puts each group's week on one card in your chat: a preview of every post, a
**Remove** per post, and one **Confirm**. Nothing is scheduled until you confirm; afterwards each post
shows Scheduled, then Posted with its link, or Failed with the reason and **Retry**. **Undo** cancels a
post until 5 minutes before it goes out. A client's card also has a switch for the client's OK, which
Confirm needs, and **Make a preview to send the client** makes a PDF you send them yourself.

With nothing connected, or for what Kin can't post (a WhatsApp channel, a personal account, a Story
sticker), Social makes a ready-to-post pack: the caption to copy, the picture to save, and a reminder
at the time to post it. Ask "how did last week go?" and it answers from the numbers; its Monday
check-in opens with what worked.

## Check-ins and the weekly brief

A check-in is an agent's regular round: it looks over what needs attention and tells you what
moved. By default the Chief of staff sends you a **Morning brief** around 9:30 every day, checks in
around 15:30 on weekdays, sends an **Evening summary** of the day around 18:30 on weekdays (what got
done, what moved, what carries to tomorrow), and writes the **Weekly brief** around 9:00 on Mondays.
On Monday you get one page: whichever of the morning and weekly briefs comes first carries the week's
figures, written by Kin from its records, and the other isn't sent. On the other mornings, the
morning brief's **Money** section carries a line from Kin for each overdue invoice and each quote
with no answer for five days (a playbook can change that number): who, how much and for how long,
three of each at most, until it is settled.

Check-in times are loose: "around 9:30" means near then, not on the minute. When you give an agent
a plain time it keeps to it; say "around" or "-ish" for a loose one. The morning brief comes on a
quiet day too. The evening summary comes only on a day with something to sum up, and only that
day; another check-in with nothing to report stays quiet.

The briefs are yours to set under **Settings → Briefs** (in **Profile** on the web): which days the
morning brief comes and when, and whether and when the afternoon check-in and evening summary come.
Your quiet hours hold any of them that would land inside them; an evening summary that would reach
you after its day is over isn't sent.

A teammate who looks after clients, and has no agent of their own, gets Kin's **Morning page**
instead: on a working morning with something new about their clients, a "Good morning" page with
what needs them, their clients and what's coming up. Someone who may see Money also gets those
Money lines on it, for what they may see, every morning until settled. It comes at the start of their working hours,
or 08:00 on weekdays if they set none, with what is true at that minute; whatever stays open is also
under **Needs you** in Tasks. They change its time and days, or turn it off, under **Settings
→ Briefs**. A change counts from the next time its time comes, so a page already sent today, or a
time already past, starts tomorrow.

Owners and co-owners manage them under **Settings → Check-ins**: add one, turn one off, or **Run
now**. Or say it in a chat: "check in at 11 instead".

**Settings → Company details → Daily limit** caps how much work your agents do each day. Once
they reach it, only your urgent messages run, on the agent's usual model, until midnight.

**Settings → Company details → Stop all agents** (owners and co-owners) stops every agent at once,
for everyone: work in progress stops, scheduled work due while they are stopped is skipped, and
nothing anyone confirms goes out. Messages wait up to a day, and every chat with an agent says the
agents are stopped. **Resume** starts them again.

## Kin keeps watch

Kin keeps a list of open loops, such as an overdue invoice waiting to be paid, and looks at each one
every 45 minutes, day and night. It waits longer between looks while nothing changes and looks again
soon after the thing itself changes. Most looks only read Kin's own records, so they cost nothing.
When a loop closes, for example when the invoice is marked paid, Kin stops watching it by itself.

A record Kin watches shows one quiet line under its header: what Kin is watching for, when it last
looked and what it found, and when it looks next. **Look now** asks for a look at once without
changing when the next one comes. **Stop watching** takes it off the list, and **Watch again** puts
it back for three days after it closed or stopped.

**What Kin checked** lists every look, one day at a time, with why it ran and what it did. Looks that
found nothing new are folded into one row you can open. Open it from a watch line, or from
**Settings → What Kin checked** (on the phone, the gear on Chats). Owners and co-owners see every
watch; a teammate sees only the watches on their own things, never money.

## Recommendations

When the same hold-up keeps happening and a setting would end it, Kin suggests the change once, as a
card in your chat with the Chief of staff, so it waits in Needs you without ringing. Kin suggests:

- letting a teammate message an agent directly, when they keep asking for its work and your cards on it run out;
- raising the daily limit one step, when it is one of the choices in **Daily limit** and Kin told you on three of the last seven days that it was reached (this can cost more);
- resuming a connection that has been paused for days while your agents keep being refused on it for that pause, unless something else would still stop it: an error, a sign-in, changes waiting for your look, or no tool switched on;
- resuming an agent that has been paused for days with work queued for it since, unless Kin is stopped for the whole company.

Nothing changes until you tap the change itself;
**Not now** keeps it quiet for 30 days, then 90, then for good. Kin suggests at most one thing a day, never in your
quiet hours, never while Needs you is full, and not in a company's first two weeks.

**Settings → Recommendations**, under Company (the owner only), lists every suggestion and what came of it, with
**Ask again** for one you put off. A change you made from a suggestion is changed back where it lives: a done row
opens that place, the agent's page, **Daily limit**, or the connection.

## Memory

**Settings → Memory** (owner and co-owners) shows what your agents have learned working with you: preferences,
decisions and lasting facts. Each item is **Only you**, **The team can know**, or **Waiting for your
yes** when an agent thinks the team could know it. Anything about access, money or client terms
stays with the owner. **Forget** an item and agents stop using it; bring it back from **Forgotten**.

Agents use what the team can know in chats with teammates only while **Agents use team memory in
chats with teammates** is on, at the top of the page. It starts off on a new Kin, and a change applies from the
next reply. Nothing under **Only you** reaches a teammate's chat either way.

### Finding a decision again

Kin keeps a decision as soon as it's made: when you or a co-owner answer an agent's question card
(a plan, a choice), and when a Team-room discussion ends decided. It is saved in the card's or the
outcome's own words, with who decided and when, and is **Only you** until you let the team know.

To find one months later, open **Search** (the magnifier on Tasks, Money, Clients or Docs, or the
command palette) and type a word from it. Decisions show as **Remembered**; open one to see it in
Settings → Memory. Search also finds invoices, quotes, expenses and receipts. Teammates search the
same box: they find the money entries the Money place shows them and never what memory keeps.

## Running Kin on your own host

Kin runs inside the T3 Code server on the owner's computer, and its data stays there. Teammates
reach it the way any remote client does: T3 Connect, your local network, or Tailscale. See
[Remote access](./remote-access.md). Keep the computer on and awake while the team works; see
[Run T3 Code in the background](./background-service.md). Make join links from the address
teammates will use.

To give agents characters you wrote yourself, see
[Setting agents' characters from the host](../operations/kin-characters.md).

### Taking your data out

**Settings → Company details → Download everything** (the owner only) saves the company as one zip. On the phone,
the same row downloads the zip and opens the share sheet.

Your clients, contacts, projects, every list with its entries and their payments, each entry's
comments, the contact logged with each client, each doc as it reads now, and the files entries,
comments and docs hold, in one zip. Kin's import makes the clients, contacts, projects and entries
again; the comments, logged contact, docs and files are there to keep.

It is not a full backup. Not in it: memory, which stays in your memory repository; chats; people
and their access; agents and their characters; check-ins and reminders; connections, social
accounts and their posts; budgets, bank statements and company details; settings; project repos
and members; and who holds each entry, how it repeats and its history. Keys, tokens, sign-in
sessions, pairing codes and bank details never leave.

`kin.jsonl` inside it is the file [Kin's import](./kin-import.md) reads. On the computer running a
new Kin, unzip the file, then:

```sh
t3 kin import --file kin.jsonl          # what it would make
t3 kin import --file kin.jsonl --apply
```

What comes back is owned by whoever imports it.
